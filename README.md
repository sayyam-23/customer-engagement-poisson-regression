# Customer Engagement Analysis: Poisson Regression

**Independent data analytics portfolio project**

This project uses exploratory data analysis and count regression to examine which customer characteristics are associated with monthly website-visit frequency.

## Research question

Which customer profile characteristics are associated with how frequently customers visit a company's website?

The outcome is `NumWebVisitsMonth`, a non-negative count. The main Poisson model focuses on income and the number of children and teenagers in the household. The analysis emphasizes interpretation, diagnostics and honest evaluation rather than adding predictors for complexity.

## Key findings

- The dataset contains **2,240 customers** and **29 variables**. Customer IDs are unique; 24 income values are missing.
- A **10% higher income near the training-sample median** is associated with about **4.0% fewer expected monthly visits** (IRR 0.960; HC3 95% CI approximately 0.956–0.965).
- **One additional child or teenager at home** is associated with about **21.3% more expected monthly visits** (IRR 1.213; HC3 95% CI approximately 1.185–1.241).
- On 448 held-out customers, the Poisson model achieves **MAE 1.546**, compared with **1.979** for an intercept-only benchmark (a 21.9% reduction).
- Repeated five-fold cross-validation within the training sample gives mean MAE **1.548** for Poisson and **2.006** for the baseline.
- Conditional dispersion ratios are below 1 (Pearson/df 0.710; deviance/df 0.776). A generalized Poisson model estimates negative dispersion and lower in-sample AIC, but its held-out MAE (1.547) is virtually identical to Poisson.

These are adjusted associations in an observational dataset, not causal effects.

## Workflow

1. Audit the data and examine monthly website-visit counts.
2. Explore descriptive relationships with customer and household characteristics.
3. Split the data into training and test samples before imputation or model fitting.
4. Fit a two-predictor Poisson regression using log-transformed income and total children/teenagers at home.
5. Check alternative specifications, dispersion, a generalized Poisson comparison, held-out performance, calibration and sensitivity to the high-income record.
6. Interpret the findings in a business context and document limitations.

## Limitations

- The dataset is observational; regression estimates do not establish causation.
- The exact reference date for monthly website visits is not documented.
- The raw data has three unusually early birth years and one exceptionally high income value. The income record is retained in the main model and checked in a sensitivity analysis. Birth year is not used to calculate age because no reliable age reference date is given.
- The holdout and cross-validation assess performance across customers in this dataset, not forecasting into a future period.
- Model calibration is imperfect and the Poisson variance assumption is not exact.
- Findings may not generalize to other companies, customer populations or time periods.

This project demonstrates an interpretable count-regression workflow; it is not a production-ready customer scoring system.
